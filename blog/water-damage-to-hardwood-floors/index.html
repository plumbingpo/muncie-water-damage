Water Damage to Hardwood Floors in Muncie, IN

Home / Blog / Saving Water-Damaged Hardwood Floors in Muncie, IN

Few parts of a house are worth as much as its hardwood flooring, and few are as easily harmed by water. Cupping, crowning, buckling, staining, and mold in the subfloor beneath are all results of water reaching hardwood. Homeowners across Muncie, IN who have wood floors benefit from knowing the right response to water and the difference between a floor worth restoring and one that needs replacing.

Why Hardwood Reacts to Water

Because wood is hygroscopic, it takes in moisture and gives it back as the conditions around it change. When hardwood floors are exposed to excessive water, the wood fibers absorb moisture and expand. The earliest sign you can see of a moisture imbalance is cupping, where board edges sit higher than the middle. If moisture exposure continues, boards may crown (center rising higher than edges as the top dries faster than the bottom) or buckle (lifting entirely off the subfloor). When a floor stays wet a long time, mold can also develop under the boards, on the subfloor, unnoticed until they are pulled.

Can Water-Damaged Hardwood Be Saved?

Five things decide if the floor is salvageable: the speed of the professional drying response, how much water there was, the time the boards spent wet, what kind of hardwood and finish you have, and what shape the subfloor is in. Floors exposed to clean water can often be saved when professional drying starts within 24 to 48 hours. What makes it work is slow, controlled drying, so moisture leaves the wood evenly and the boards do not split or crack.

How Professionals Dry Hardwood Floors

Professional hardwood floor drying uses specialized techniques that are different from standard structural drying. To pull moisture evenly from the wood, mats or panels go directly on the surface and create a controlled drying environment. Floor drying systems can also be used beneath the subfloor from crawl spaces. Slow and even is the rule: rush it and the surface dries ahead of the core, leaving permanent splits, cracks, and cupping.

When Replacement Is Necessary

As a rule, hardwood has to be replaced after more than 48 to 72 hours of being wet, after contact with contaminated water such as floodwater or sewage, when delamination or buckling is widespread, when mold is growing on the subfloor, or when the subfloor has itself been damaged.
