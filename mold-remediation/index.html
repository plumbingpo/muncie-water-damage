What a Professional Brings to Mold Remediation

Mold remediation is not a DIY project. Disturbing mold colonies without proper containment sends millions of spores airborne, which can contaminate unaffected areas of your home and create worse health exposure than the original problem. Capturing and containing the spores during removal takes HEPA-filtered scrubbers, protective equipment for the crew and containment kept under negative air pressure, which is how professionals do it.

A professional job goes after the moisture source too, because that is why the mold grew in the first place. If the moisture is left alone, taking away what you can see only buys time, and regrowth often shows up within weeks. Our certified technicians are trained and equipped to track down moisture you cannot see, dry the affected areas the right way and make the corrections that keep mold from recurring.

How to Prevent Mold Remediation Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly mold issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Use ventilation and a dehumidifier to hold humidity under 60%, with 30 to 50% as the ideal
  • Mold can get going after just 24 to 48 hours of dampness, so have leaks fixed within 24 hours
  • Use mold-resistant drywall and paint in moisture-prone areas like bathrooms and basements
  • Laundry rooms, baths and kitchens all need working exhaust ventilation that is sized for the space
  • Do not leave damp laundry, bath mats or spilled water sitting for long
  • Deal quickly with condensation that forms on pipes, cold surfaces or window glass

Signs It Is Time for Mold Remediation

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Black, green, white or other colored growth you can see on surfaces in the building
  • Cleaning that never lasts, with growth returning to the same surface
  • Stains spreading on a ceiling or wall, or dark marks ringing the air vents
  • Peeling wallpaper, bubbling paint or warped materials that point to hidden moisture
  • Headaches, allergy flare-ups or breathing symptoms that ease whenever you leave the house
  • Condensation forming regularly on windows, walls or cold surfaces
  • Water damage not long ago that was left to dry without professional equipment
