Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Muncie

A water heater holds 40 to 80 gallons, but that is not the limit of the leak. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

Our Muncie team extracts the water, then looks at what surrounded the heater. Usually the tank platform and the lower part of the closet walls have taken on the most water. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Attic leaks travel downward through the insulation and the ceilings below. Each of those areas is metered, then dried using dehumidifiers and air movers.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Never leave a heating element or burner running on an empty tank.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveTurn the valve on the cold pipe above the heater clockwise. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterTank water is hot enough to scald. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyIf you can reach the drain valve, attach a garden hose and run it outside or to a floor drain so the remaining water does not empty onto the floor.
  5. Move Stored ItemsGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • Quantity of insulation and drywall, on walls or ceilings, that must come out.
  • Amount of contents affected, since wet belongings have to be relocated, dried or discarded.
  • Time that passed with the tank still refilling, up to the point the supply was shut.
  • If water got to the flooring of adjoining rooms, and the kind of flooring involved.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • An overflow pan that cannot drain, because its line is clogged or the pan itself has rusted.
  • A missing expansion tank or a failed thermostat that lets too much pressure build inside.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
What Is at Risk

What the Water Damages

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Heat makes it worse, with quicker swelling of wood boards and carpet backing that comes apart.

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Stored Belongings

Boxes, furniture and keepsakes stored near a garage or closet heater sit in the water first. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Heater Closet Drywall

Drywall surrounding a heater closet inside the house wicks water up and lets it through to the neighboring rooms. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.
